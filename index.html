#### The maps below are in timeline order, with the older maps being displayed first and the more recent ones being displayed further down. ### Below are various OBXFS 2025 Capstone Maps. These maps were created in the context of the 2025 Capstone Report, titled 'Patterns of Protection' that was presented to over 100 community members and scientific professionals at the Coastal Studies Institute in Wanchese, North Carolina. The YouTube link is provided here (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qpldcU6y1Bw&t=448s) if you would like to watch the entire presentation. #### The map below is the map I created for the introduction section of our report. The three important coastal counties needed (Date, Hyde, and Currituck) are plotted, and the smaller inset map represents our study area. This map was created with QGIS using NC OneMap data. #### The map below is a map of the study sites that were sampled throughout the course of the capstone in Nags Head Woods. There were three longitudinal dunes sampled, one on the northern side of the forest (N), one on the southern (S) and one in the middle (M); the two ends of these dunes sampled were classified as 'sound' or 'ocean' side, depending on their relative location to the Albemarle-Pamlico Sound to the West and the Atlantic Ocean to the East. These classifications are reflected in the labels. The elevation raster placed underneath the sites makes it obvious that they are areas of high relative elevation to the remainder of the coastal area. This map was made in QGIS, and the data was gleaned from our capstone report and the NC OneMap Service. #### The map below is a map of the important infrastructure in the area in the Nags Head Woods Nature Preserve. The boundary of Nags Head Woods is denoted in the hashed black line, and the extensive trail system that runs throughout the Woods is modeled in the distinct colors. The inset maps denote the NHW Preserve House and the Visitors Center. This map was made in QGIS, and the data was gleaned from NC OneMap and Lora Eddy, the Conservation Coordinator and Resilience Specialist at The Nature Conservancy (TNC). #### The map below is a representation of the management and stewardship responsibilities of each of the distinct regions of the Nags Head Woods Nature Preserve. Interestingly, it is split up among the Town of Kill Devil Hills, the Town of Nags Head, and The Nature Conservancy. This map was made with QGIS and the data was provided by Lora Eddy of TNC. #### Below is the study site map created for my summer research project. This map reflects the five study sites sampled during June 2026. The sites that were previously sampled and established are denoted with purple stars, and the northern sites I established are denoted with the blue triangles. Pink circles represent the closest NOAA water quality stations to my study sites. This map reflects the spatial relationship of these sites to each other and the broader Outer Banks region. ArcGIS Pro was used to create this map, and data was gleaned from Hannah Palko (2017, 2026) and myself (2026). ### Below are the GEOG 455 maps created. #### The Portland metropolitan area is outlined in purple on the map below. 450 foot contour lines are depicted around the Digital Elevation Model (DEM) and the hillshade, which are placed as terrain layers.
